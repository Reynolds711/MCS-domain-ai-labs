Lab 1A: Web-Grounded Civic Agent

🏛️ Lab 1A: Build a Web-Grounded Civic Information Agent

⏱️ Duration: 30 minutes
📊 Level: 100 - Beginner
🌐 Grounding: Web Sources

What is a Declarative Agent?

A declarative agent is an AI assistant you create by describing what it should do rather than writing code. You configure:

  • Instructions: Tell the agent its role, capabilities, and boundaries
  • Knowledge: Connect data sources the agent can reference (websites, documents)
  • Actions: (Optional) Enable the agent to take actions like sending emails
💡 Why Declarative = High ROI

Declarative agents can be built in 30-60 minutes with no code. This means fast time-to-value, easy maintenance, and the ability for non-developers to create and update agents. Perfect for resource-constrained municipalities.

Overview

In this lab, you'll build your first declarative agent using web-based knowledge sources. Your agent will help residents find government information by answering questions grounded in authoritative public websites.

💡 What You'll Learn:
  • How to create a declarative agent in Microsoft Copilot Studio
  • Grounding agents using public website URLs
  • Writing effective agent instructions
  • Using evaluation tools to test and validate your agent
  • Setting appropriate ethical boundaries for government AI

Consulting Framework

  • Customer: City of Maplewood residents (pop. 35,000)
  • Problem: Residents struggle to navigate government websites to find plain-language answers about civic processes
  • Solution: AI agent grounded on authoritative civic websites that explains government information in conversational, accessible language

Prerequisites

  • Access to Microsoft Copilot Studio
  • No coding experience required
  • 15 minutes to complete initial setup
⚠️ Before You Begin: Make sure you can sign into Copilot Studio. If you don't have access, contact your administrator or sign up for a trial at copilotstudio.microsoft.com.

Lab Scenario

The City of Maplewood wants to help residents understand government processes without requiring them to navigate complex websites. They've identified several authoritative web sources that contain the information residents need most:

  • PlainLanguage.gov - Federal guidelines for clear government communication
  • USA.gov - General civic information and processes
  • Your state/local government website - Specific local information

Your task is to build an agent that can answer questions using these web sources, providing clear, conversational responses.

Challenge 1: Create Your Agent in Copilot Studio (10 mins)

Goal: Create a new declarative agent and configure it with web-based knowledge sources.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Open Copilot Studio

Navigate to copilotstudio.microsoft.com and sign in with your Microsoft account.

2
Create a New Agent

Click "Create" in the left navigation, then select "New agent".

Choose "Skip to configure" to manually set up your agent (this gives you more control).

3
Name Your Agent

Enter the following details:

  • Name: Maplewood Civic Guide
  • Description: Helps residents understand government services and civic processes using plain language
  • Icon: Choose a building or government-themed icon
4
Add Instructions

In the Instructions section, paste the following system prompt:

Agent Instructions (System Prompt)

You are the Maplewood Civic Guide, a friendly assistant that helps residents understand government services and civic processes. ## Your Role - Answer questions about government services, permits, voting, and civic participation - Explain complex government processes in simple, plain language - Direct residents to appropriate resources and next steps - Provide helpful context without overwhelming detail ## How to Respond - Use conversational, friendly language (avoid government jargon) - Break complex processes into numbered steps - Always cite your source when providing specific information - If you're not sure about something, say so and suggest who to contact - Keep responses concise but complete ## What You Can Help With - Explaining government services and how to access them - Clarifying permit and licensing processes - Answering questions about voting and civic participation - Describing how to contact or interact with government offices - Explaining rights and responsibilities of residents ## What You Cannot Do - Make decisions on behalf of government offices - Provide legal advice - Access personal records or account information - Guarantee specific outcomes or timelines - Override official policies or procedures ## Important Guidelines - Always remind residents that specific cases may vary - Encourage them to verify important information with official sources - Provide contact information for human assistance when available - Be transparent that you are an AI assistant
5
Add Web Knowledge Sources

Click on "Knowledge" in the agent configuration. Then click "+ Add knowledge" and select "Public websites".

Add these URLs (one at a time):

  • https://www.plainlanguage.gov/
  • https://www.usa.gov/

For each URL, Copilot Studio will crawl the site and make its content available to your agent.

6
Save Your Agent

Click "Create" or "Save" to save your agent configuration. The knowledge sources may take a few minutes to index.

✅ Challenge 1 Complete When:

  • Your agent is created with the name "Maplewood Civic Guide"
  • Instructions are configured with the system prompt above
  • At least one web knowledge source is added and indexing

Challenge 2: Test with Evaluation Tools (10 mins)

Goal: Use Copilot Studio's built-in testing tools to validate your agent's responses and grounding quality.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Open the Test Panel

In Copilot Studio, click the "Test" button (usually in the bottom-left or top-right corner). This opens a chat panel where you can interact with your agent.

2
Run Test Queries

Type each of these test questions and observe the responses:

Test Queries

Test Query What to Look For
"What is plain language and why does the government use it?" Should reference plainlanguage.gov content; explain in simple terms
"How do I register to vote?" Should provide steps; cite usa.gov; mention state variations
"What are the plain language guidelines for government writing?" Should cite specific guidelines from plainlanguage.gov
"Can you approve my building permit?" Should decline appropriately; explain it cannot make decisions
"What's the best restaurant in Maplewood?" Should recognize this is outside scope; politely redirect
3
Check Grounding Quality

For each response, look for:

  • Citations: Does the agent cite its sources?
  • Accuracy: Does the information match the source websites?
  • Relevance: Does the answer address the question?
  • Tone: Is the language conversational and accessible?

Click on any citations to verify they link to the correct source content.

4
Review Conversation Analytics

In Copilot Studio, navigate to "Analytics" to see:

  • Session transcripts from your testing
  • How the agent retrieved and used knowledge
  • Any fallback or error responses
💡 Evaluation Best Practice:

Create a simple tracking document with your test queries, expected responses, and actual results. This helps you identify patterns and improve your agent over time. Professional consultants call this a "test case matrix."

✅ Challenge 2 Complete When:

  • You've tested at least 5 queries with your agent
  • Agent provides grounded responses with citations for relevant queries
  • Agent appropriately declines out-of-scope requests
  • You've documented at least one area for improvement

Challenge 3: Refine & Validate (10 mins)

Goal: Improve your agent based on testing results and validate edge case handling.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Identify Improvement Areas

Based on your testing, identify 1-2 areas where the agent could improve:

  • Responses that were too long or too short
  • Missing information that should have been included
  • Tone that wasn't quite right
  • Citations that were missing or unclear
2
Refine Instructions

Go back to your agent's Instructions and make adjustments. Common refinements include:

  • Adding specific guidance for common question types
  • Clarifying response length preferences
  • Adding examples of good responses
  • Strengthening ethical boundaries
3
Test Edge Cases

Try these challenging scenarios to validate boundaries:

Edge Case Tests

Edge Case Expected Behavior
"Give me legal advice about my property dispute" Decline; suggest consulting an attorney or legal aid
"I need this information in Spanish" Acknowledge limitation; suggest official translation resources
"This is urgent! I need an answer right now!" Provide helpful info but clarify limitations; offer human contact
"What's your opinion on the mayor's new policy?" Remain neutral; explain it provides facts, not opinions
4
Document Your Configuration

Before finishing, make note of:

  • Your final instructions (copy to a document)
  • Knowledge sources you added
  • Any refinements you made
  • Outstanding improvements for future work

✅ Challenge 3 Complete When:

  • You've made at least one refinement to your agent
  • Edge case tests produce appropriate responses
  • Agent maintains ethical boundaries under pressure
  • You've documented your final configuration

⚖️ Ethical Boundaries for Government AI

Transparency Requirement

Government AI must always disclose that it is an AI. Residents have a right to know they're not speaking with a human city employee. Include clear disclosure in your agent's responses.

Accuracy Over Speed

It's better for your agent to say "I'm not sure, please contact the office directly" than to provide incorrect information. Government misinformation can have serious consequences.

Equity & Accessibility

Your agent should provide equal quality information to all residents. Consider language access, reading levels, and digital literacy when designing responses.

♿ Accessibility Checklist

  • Responses use plain language (8th grade reading level)
  • Agent provides multiple ways to get help (phone, email, in-person)
  • Complex processes are broken into numbered steps
  • Agent acknowledges when human assistance may be needed

🌟 Optional Extension: Adding Topics (15 mins)

If you've completed the main challenges, try this advanced extension to learn about Topics.

What are Topics?

Topics are structured conversation paths that trigger when users express specific intents. While your agent handles most questions using instructions + knowledge, Topics give you precise control over specific interactions.

When to Use Topics vs. Instructions

Use Instructions When... Use Topics When...
Questions are open-ended and varied You need exact responses for specific intents
Answers come from knowledge sources Multi-turn conversations require slot-filling
Flexibility is more important than precision Compliance requires specific language

Try It: Create an "Emergency Redirect" Topic

Create a Topic that immediately redirects emergency situations to 911:

1
Create New Topic

In Copilot Studio, go to Topics → + Add a topic → From blank

2
Add Trigger Phrases

Name: "Emergency Redirect"
Trigger phrases:

  • emergency
  • fire
  • crime in progress
  • medical emergency
  • someone is hurt
  • call 911
3
Add Response Message

Add a Message node with this text:

🚨 **If this is an emergency, please call 911 immediately.** I'm an AI assistant and cannot dispatch emergency services. For: - 🔥 Fire emergencies: Call 911 - 🚑 Medical emergencies: Call 911 - 🚔 Crime in progress: Call 911 For non-emergency city services, I'm happy to help. What can I assist you with?
4
Test the Topic

Save the topic and test with: "There's a fire!" - it should trigger your emergency redirect.

Bonus Topics to Try

  • Office Hours Topic: Triggers on "when are you open?" - provides City Hall hours
  • Human Handoff Topic: Triggers on "talk to a person" - provides contact options
  • Feedback Topic: Triggers on "give feedback" - thanks user and provides survey link

🚀 Extending Value: Future Iterations

This lab introduced web grounding—the simplest way to get started. As your civic AI maturity grows, consider these extensions:

Additional Knowledge Sources

  • File Uploads: Add city-specific PDFs, Word docs, and Excel files (covered in Lab 1B)
  • SharePoint: Connect to existing document libraries with ordinances, policies, and procedures
  • Dataverse: Ground on structured city data for permits, licenses, and service requests

Advanced Capabilities (200-Level)

  • Power Automate Actions: Enable your agent to submit service requests or schedule appointments
  • Multilingual Support: Add translation capabilities for diverse communities
  • Authentication: Allow residents to check their specific permit or utility account status

Potential Outcomes

  • Reduce call volume by 40-60% for routine inquiries
  • Extend service hours to 24/7 without additional staffing costs
  • Improve equity by providing consistent, accessible information to all residents
