🏛️ Lab 1B: Build a File-Grounded Services Agent

⏱️ Duration: 30 minutes
📊 Level: 100 - Beginner
📄 Grounding: File Sources

Building on Lab 1A

In Lab 1A, you created an agent grounded on public websites. In this lab, you'll learn to ground agents on local files—documents that contain your organization's specific information.

Web Grounding vs. File Grounding

Web Grounding (Lab 1A) File Grounding (Lab 1B)
Uses public website URLs Uses uploaded documents (Word, Excel, PDF)
Content updates automatically as sites change You control exactly what content is included
Good for general public information Good for organization-specific data
Limited control over content quality Full control over content accuracy
💡 Real-World Use Case:

Most enterprise scenarios use file grounding because organizations have proprietary information stored in SharePoint, file shares, or document management systems. This lab teaches you to work with the file types you'll encounter in actual deployments.

Overview

In this lab, you'll build a declarative agent grounded on local document files that contain city services information. You'll use provided sample files that mirror real municipal documents: a services guide, permit matrix, and department directory.

💡 What You'll Learn:
  • How to prepare documents for AI grounding
  • Uploading and indexing file-based knowledge sources
  • Differences between document formats (PDF, Word, Excel)
  • Testing grounding accuracy with file sources
  • Evaluating citation quality for document references

Consulting Framework

  • Customer: City of Maplewood staff and residents
  • Problem: City services information is scattered across multiple documents; residents and staff struggle to find answers
  • Solution: AI agent that searches across city documents and provides instant answers with citations to source documents

Lab Scenario

The City of Maplewood has created comprehensive documentation for their services, but residents still call with basic questions because they can't find information in lengthy documents. The city wants an AI agent that can:

  • Search across all city documents instantly
  • Answer questions using specific document content
  • Cite the exact source document and section
  • Handle the specific services, permits, and contacts Maplewood offers

Challenge 1: Prepare and Upload Knowledge Files (10 mins)

Goal: Create a new agent and configure it with file-based knowledge sources.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Review the Lab Files

Before uploading, open each downloaded file and review its structure:

  • City Services Guide: Comprehensive service descriptions, eligibility, fees
  • Permit Matrix: Structured table of permit types, requirements, costs
  • Department Directory: Contact info organized by department

Understanding your source content helps you write better agent instructions.

2
Create a New Agent

In Copilot Studio, create a new agent:

  • Name: Maplewood Services Assistant
  • Description: Helps residents and staff find information about city services, permits, and department contacts
3
Add Instructions

Configure the agent with these instructions:

Agent Instructions (System Prompt)

You are the Maplewood Services Assistant, helping residents and city staff find information about city services, permits, and department contacts. ## Your Knowledge Sources You have access to three key city documents: 1. **City Services Guide** - Descriptions of all services, eligibility, fees, and how to apply 2. **Permit Requirements Matrix** - Specific permits, required documents, fees, and processing times 3. **Department Directory** - Contact information for all city departments and key staff ## How to Respond - Always search your knowledge sources before answering - Cite the specific document and section where you found information - If information exists in multiple documents, synthesize it helpfully - Use exact figures for fees, timeframes, and requirements (don't approximate) - Format complex information with bullet points or numbered steps ## Response Format When answering questions: 1. Provide the direct answer first 2. Include specific details (fees, timeframes, documents needed) 3. Cite your source: "According to the [Document Name]..." 4. Offer related helpful information if relevant 5. Provide contact information for follow-up ## Handling Questions Outside Your Knowledge If a question isn't covered in your documents: - Clearly state that this information isn't in your current knowledge - Suggest the most relevant department to contact - Never make up information that isn't in your sources ## What You Cannot Do - Approve permits or applications - Access personal resident accounts - Make exceptions to stated policies - Provide legal or financial advice - Guarantee specific outcomes ## Important Reminders - Fees and requirements may change; encourage residents to verify for large projects - Always provide a phone number or contact for complex situations - Be helpful but direct residents to staff for anything requiring judgment
4
Upload Knowledge Files

Go to Knowledge → + Add knowledge → Files

Upload each of the three lab files. Copilot Studio will index them for search.

Tip: You can also use SharePoint or OneDrive as sources if your files are stored there.

5
Wait for Indexing

File indexing typically takes 1-3 minutes. You'll see a status indicator showing when files are ready.

✅ Challenge 1 Complete When:

  • Agent is created with the name "Maplewood Services Assistant"
  • All three knowledge files are uploaded and indexed
  • Instructions are configured with the system prompt above

Goal: Validate that your agent correctly retrieves and cites information from your uploaded documents.

Test Queries - Permit Matrix

These questions should be answered using the Permit Requirements Matrix:

Test Query Expected Source Key Information to Include
"What do I need for a building permit?" Permit Matrix - Building Permit row Required documents, fee, processing time
"How much does a fence permit cost?" Permit Matrix - Fence Permit row Exact fee amount, when permits are required
"Do I need a permit for a garage sale?" Permit Matrix - Special permits section Garage sale permit requirements or exemptions

Test Queries - Department Directory

These questions should be answered using the Department Directory:

Test Query Expected Source Key Information to Include
"Who do I contact about my water bill?" Department Directory - Utilities Contact name, phone, email, hours
"What's the phone number for the building department?" Department Directory - Building & Zoning Phone number, address, hours
"Who is the city manager?" Department Directory - Administration Name, contact information

Evaluation Checklist

For each response, verify:

  • ☐ Answer includes information from the correct document
  • ☐ Specific figures (fees, phone numbers) are accurate
  • ☐ Response cites the source document
  • ☐ Information is complete (doesn't omit important details)
  • ☐ Format is clear and easy to read

✅ Challenge 2 Complete When:

  • Tested at least 2 queries per document source (6 total)
  • Agent cites the correct source document for each answer
  • Specific figures (fees, phone numbers) are accurate
  • You've documented any inaccuracies for refinement

Challenge 3: Cross-Document Queries & Edge Cases (10 mins)

Goal: Test advanced scenarios where the agent must synthesize information from multiple documents or handle questions outside its knowledge.

Cross-Document Test Queries

These questions require information from multiple documents:

Test Query Expected Synthesis
"I want to add a deck to my house. What permit do I need and who do I contact?" Permit Matrix (deck permit requirements) + Department Directory (Building Dept contact)
"What recreation programs are available and how do I register?" Services Guide (programs) + Department Directory (Parks Dept contact) + possibly Services Guide (registration process)
"I want to open a business in Maplewood. What do I need to do?" Permit Matrix (business license) + Services Guide (business services) + Department Directory (contacts)

Edge Case Tests

Test how the agent handles challenging scenarios:

Edge Case Expected Behavior
"What's the mayor's email address?" (may not be in docs) Check knowledge first; if not found, admit it and suggest Administration contact
"Can you waive the permit fee for me?" Decline; explain it cannot make exceptions; provide staff contact
"The permit fee on your website is different than what you said" Acknowledge discrepancy possibility; recommend calling to verify current fees
"Is my permit application approved?" Explain it cannot access personal records; provide contact to check status

Refine Based on Results

If you find issues in testing, consider these refinements:

  • Missing citations: Add instruction to always cite sources
  • Incomplete answers: Add instruction to check multiple documents for comprehensive answers
  • Wrong information: Check that files uploaded correctly; verify content is in documents
  • Poor formatting: Add formatting guidance in instructions

✅ Challenge 3 Complete When:

  • Agent successfully synthesizes information from multiple documents
  • Edge cases are handled appropriately (declines what it can't do)
  • You've made at least one refinement based on testing
  • Agent provides helpful alternatives when it can't answer

⚖️ Ethical Boundaries for Document-Grounded Agents

Document Accuracy Responsibility

When using file grounding, you're responsible for the accuracy of your source documents. Unlike web grounding (where content is maintained by others), file content is under your control. Establish a process to keep documents current.

Citation Transparency

File-grounded agents should always cite which document information came from. This helps users verify information and builds trust. It also helps you identify when documents need updating.

Handling Conflicts

If documents contain conflicting information (e.g., an old fee in one document, new fee in another), the agent should acknowledge the conflict and recommend contacting staff. Train your agent to handle this gracefully.

🌟 Optional Extension: Service-Specific Topics (15 mins)

Create Topics for high-frequency, structured interactions that benefit from precise control.

Topic Idea 1: Permit Lookup Wizard

Create a multi-turn conversation that guides users through permit selection:

Topic: Permit Finder Trigger phrases: "what permit do I need", "help me find a permit", "permit lookup" Flow: 1. Ask: "What type of project are you planning?" - Options: Home improvement, Business, Event, Other 2. Based on answer, ask follow-up: - Home improvement → "What kind of work?" (Building, Electrical, Plumbing, Fence/Deck) - Business → "Is this a new business or existing?" 3. Provide specific permit information from knowledge base 4. Include: requirements, fee, contact information 5. End with: "Would you like me to explain the application process?"

Topic Idea 2: Department Finder

Quick routing to the right department:

Topic: Department Finder Trigger phrases: "who do I call", "find a department", "I need to talk to someone about" Flow: 1. Ask: "What topic do you need help with?" - Categories: Permits & Zoning, Utilities, Streets & Roads, Parks, Police/Fire, Other 2. Retrieve department info from Directory 3. Provide: Name, phone, email, hours, location 4. Ask: "Can I help with anything else about [topic]?"

Topic Idea 3: Fee Calculator

Structured fee lookup with clear outputs:

Topic: Fee Calculator Trigger phrases: "how much does it cost", "what's the fee for", "permit cost" Flow: 1. Ask: "What service or permit are you asking about?" 2. Search Permit Matrix and Services Guide for fees 3. Present in clear format: - Fee amount - What's included - Payment methods accepted - Any additional fees that may apply 4. Caveat: "Fees are subject to change. Verify with the department for large projects."
