## 1. WRITING RUBRIC (GENERAL)

### Purpose
Use this rubric for general writing assignments that don't fit a specific genre. Adaptable for any subject area.

### Scoring Levels
- **4 - Exceeds Expectations:** Demonstrates mastery beyond grade-level expectations
- **3 - Meets Expectations:** Demonstrates solid grade-level proficiency
- **2 - Approaching Expectations:** Demonstrates partial understanding, needs support
- **1 - Below Expectations:** Demonstrates limited understanding, significant support needed

### Criteria

| Criteria | 4 - Exceeds | 3 - Meets | 2 - Approaching | 1 - Below |
|----------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Focus & Organization** | Clear thesis/focus maintained throughout; logical organization enhances meaning; sophisticated transitions | Clear thesis/focus; logical organization; effective transitions | Thesis/focus present but inconsistent; some organizational issues; basic transitions | No clear thesis/focus; disorganized; lacks transitions |
| **Development & Evidence** | Thorough development with compelling evidence; insightful analysis; goes beyond requirements | Adequate development with relevant evidence; clear analysis | Limited development; evidence present but weak; surface-level analysis | Minimal development; little or no evidence; no analysis |
| **Voice & Audience Awareness** | Distinctive voice; highly engaging; expertly tailored to audience | Appropriate voice; engages reader; addresses audience | Inconsistent voice; limited engagement; audience awareness weak | No clear voice; does not engage; ignores audience |
| **Language & Conventions** | Sophisticated vocabulary; varied sentence structure; virtually no errors | Grade-appropriate vocabulary; sentence variety; few errors | Basic vocabulary; simple sentences; errors interfere sometimes | Limited vocabulary; choppy sentences; errors interfere significantly |

### Total Points
- 16 points possible
- 14-16 = A (Exceeds)
- 11-13 = B (Meets)
- 8-10 = C (Approaching)
- Below 8 = Needs Revision

## 2. ARGUMENTATIVE WRITING RUBRIC

### Purpose
Use this rubric specifically for argumentative essays and persuasive writing aligned to CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.6-8.1

### Criteria

| Criteria | 4 - Exceeds | 3 - Meets | 2 - Approaching | 1 - Below |
|----------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Claim** | Clear, compelling claim that takes a defensible position; addresses complexity of the issue | Clear claim that takes a position; addresses the issue | Claim present but weak or unclear; position not fully developed | No clear claim; position cannot be identified |
| **Evidence** | Multiple pieces of strong, credible evidence; evidence clearly supports claim; properly cited | Adequate evidence that supports claim; mostly credible; cited | Limited evidence; some may not clearly support claim; citation issues | Little or no evidence; evidence doesn't support claim; no citations |
| **Reasoning** | Thorough explanation of how evidence supports claim; addresses counterarguments effectively | Explains connection between evidence and claim; acknowledges counterarguments | Limited reasoning; weak connection to claim; counterarguments ignored | No reasoning provided; no connection made; counterarguments not addressed |
| **Organization** | Introduction hooks reader and presents claim; body paragraphs logically organized; conclusion extends thinking | Clear introduction with claim; organized body paragraphs; effective conclusion | Introduction and conclusion present but weak; some organizational issues | Missing or ineffective introduction/conclusion; disorganized |
| **Language & Conventions** | Formal academic tone; sophisticated vocabulary; virtually no errors | Appropriate formal tone; grade-level vocabulary; few errors | Inconsistent tone; basic vocabulary; some errors | Informal tone; limited vocabulary; frequent errors |

### Total Points
- 20 points possible
- 18-20 = A
- 15-17 = B
- 12-14 = C
- Below 12 = Needs Revision

### Assessment Notes
- **Claim** is weighted most heavily - if student has no clear claim, maximum score is "Approaching"
- For ELL students: Focus feedback on Claim, Evidence, and Reasoning before Language conventions

## 3. NARRATIVE WRITING RUBRIC

### Purpose
Use this rubric for narrative writing assignments including personal narratives, short stories, and creative writing aligned to CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.6-8.3

### Criteria

| Criteria | 4 - Exceeds | 3 - Meets | 2 - Approaching | 1 - Below |
|----------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Narrative Structure** | Engages reader from opening; clear sequence of events; satisfying resolution; pacing enhances story | Clear beginning, middle, end; events in logical sequence; resolution present; appropriate pacing | Structure present but weak; some sequencing issues; resolution rushed; pacing problems | No clear structure; events confusing; no resolution |
| **Character Development** | Vivid, complex characters; characters grow/change; dialogue reveals character | Characters described; some development; dialogue present and appropriate | Characters present but flat; limited development; dialogue weak or absent | Characters unclear; no development; no dialogue |
| **Setting & Description** | Rich sensory details; setting enhances mood; "show don't tell" consistently applied | Setting described; some sensory details; mostly shows rather than tells | Limited setting details; few sensory details; mostly tells rather than shows | No setting details; no sensory language |
| **Word Choice & Voice** | Precise, vivid word choices; strong narrative voice; figurative language enhances meaning | Appropriate word choices; voice present; some figurative language | Basic word choices; voice inconsistent; little figurative language | Limited vocabulary; no voice; no figurative language |
| **Conventions** | Virtually no errors; dialogue punctuated correctly; paragraphing enhances narrative | Few errors; dialogue mostly correct; appropriate paragraphing | Some errors that interfere; dialogue punctuation inconsistent; paragraphing issues | Frequent errors; dialogue incorrect; no paragraphing |

### Total Points
- 20 points possible
- 18-20 = A
- 15-17 = B
- 12-14 = C
- Below 12 = Needs Revision

## 4. RESEARCH PROJECT RUBRIC

### Purpose
Use this rubric for research-based projects including reports, presentations, and multimedia projects aligned to CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.6-8.7

### Criteria

| Criteria | 4 - Exceeds | 3 - Meets | 2 - Approaching | 1 - Below |
|----------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Research Question/Focus** | Focused, original research question; clearly defines scope; demonstrates deep inquiry | Clear research question; appropriate scope; shows genuine inquiry | Research question present but too broad/narrow; scope issues | No clear research question; unfocused |
| **Source Quality** | Multiple credible sources (5+); variety of source types; sources highly relevant | Adequate credible sources (3-4); some variety; sources relevant | Limited sources (1-2); credibility issues; relevance questionable | No credible sources; irrelevant sources |
| **Integration of Sources** | Synthesizes information across sources; draws original conclusions; avoids over-reliance on any source | Integrates information from sources; conclusions supported; balanced use | Limited integration; conclusions weak; over-relies on single source | No integration; no conclusions; copies from sources |
| **Citations** | All sources properly cited in-text and in bibliography; formatting perfect | Most sources cited; minor formatting errors | Some citations missing; formatting inconsistent | Few or no citations; no bibliography |
| **Presentation** | Professional quality; exceeds format requirements; visually/aurally engaging | Meets format requirements; organized; clear communication | Partially meets requirements; some organizational issues | Does not meet requirements; disorganized; unclear |

### Total Points
- 20 points possible
- 18-20 = A
- 15-17 = B
- 12-14 = C
- Below 12 = Needs Revision

### Source Requirements by Grade
- **Grade 6:** Minimum 3 sources
- **Grade 7:** Minimum 4 sources, at least 2 types
- **Grade 8:** Minimum 5 sources, at least 3 types

## 5. ORAL PRESENTATION RUBRIC

### Purpose
Use this rubric for speeches, oral reports, and formal presentations aligned to CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.SL.6-8.4

### Criteria

| Criteria | 4 - Exceeds | 3 - Meets | 2 - Approaching | 1 - Below |
|----------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Content & Organization** | Clear thesis; well-organized; compelling introduction and conclusion; appropriate length | Clear focus; organized; introduction and conclusion present; meets time requirement | Focus unclear at times; some organizational issues; time issues | No clear focus; disorganized; significantly over/under time |
| **Delivery - Voice** | Projects voice clearly; varied pace and tone; uses pauses effectively; no filler words | Adequate volume; appropriate pace; some variation; minimal filler words | Volume or pace issues; monotone; frequent filler words | Cannot be heard; too fast/slow; constant filler words |
| **Delivery - Body Language** | Confident posture; natural gestures; consistent eye contact with entire audience | Good posture; some gestures; makes eye contact | Posture or gesture issues; limited eye contact | Closed posture; no gestures; no eye contact |
| **Visual Aids** | Visuals enhance presentation; professional quality; integrated seamlessly | Visuals support presentation; readable; used appropriately | Visuals present but weak; hard to read; awkward use | No visuals or visuals detract from presentation |
| **Audience Engagement** | Captures and maintains audience attention; responds to audience cues; handles questions expertly | Engages audience; aware of audience; answers questions adequately | Limited engagement; ignores audience cues; struggles with questions | No engagement; ignores audience; cannot answer questions |

### Total Points
- 20 points possible
- 18-20 = A
- 15-17 = B
- 12-14 = C
- Below 12 = Needs Practice

### Time Requirements
- **6th Grade:** 3-5 minutes
- **7th Grade:** 4-6 minutes
- **8th Grade:** 5-7 minutes

## 6. GROUP COLLABORATION RUBRIC

### Purpose
Use this rubric to assess individual student contributions to group work. Each student receives an individual score.

### Criteria

| Criteria | 4 - Exceeds | 3 - Meets | 2 - Approaching | 1 - Below |
|----------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Contribution to Work** | Consistently provides high-quality contributions; often goes above requirements; helps others contribute | Provides fair share of work; meets all deadlines; contributions are quality | Contributes but inconsistently; occasionally misses deadlines; quality varies | Rarely contributes; misses deadlines; low quality work |
| **Collaboration Skills** | Actively encourages others; resolves conflicts constructively; builds on others' ideas | Works well with group; respectful; considers others' ideas | Some collaboration issues; occasional conflicts; sometimes dismissive | Cannot work with group; causes conflicts; dismisses others |
| **Communication** | Communicates ideas clearly; listens actively; provides constructive feedback | Communicates adequately; listens; gives and receives feedback | Communication issues; doesn't always listen; feedback unhelpful | Poor communication; doesn't listen; no meaningful feedback |
| **Responsibility** | Takes initiative; highly reliable; supports group success over personal recognition | Meets responsibilities; reliable; values group success | Sometimes unreliable; prioritizes self over group | Unreliable; does not meet responsibilities; hinders group |

### Total Points
- 16 points possible
- 14-16 = A
- 11-13 = B
- 8-10 = C
- Below 8 = See Teacher

### Assessment Notes
- Collect peer feedback but weight teacher observation more heavily
- Document specific observations during group work
- Meet individually with students scoring below 8

## 7. PARTICIPATION RUBRIC

### Purpose
Use this rubric for daily or weekly participation grades. Best used with student self-assessment.

### Criteria

| Criteria | 4 - Exceeds | 3 - Meets | 2 - Approaching | 1 - Below |
|----------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Engagement** | Actively engaged in all activities; volunteers regularly; asks thoughtful questions | Engaged in most activities; participates when called on; asks questions | Engaged sometimes; participates minimally; rarely asks questions | Disengaged; does not participate; no questions |
| **Preparation** | Always prepared with materials; completes all homework; often does extra preparation | Usually prepared; usually completes homework | Sometimes unprepared; homework completion inconsistent | Rarely prepared; rarely completes homework |
| **Respectful Behavior** | Consistently respectful; supports classroom community; models expected behavior | Usually respectful; follows classroom expectations | Sometimes disrespectful; needs reminders about expectations | Often disrespectful; frequently disrupts class |
| **Focus & Effort** | Stays on task independently; maximum effort consistently; perseveres through challenges | Usually on task; good effort; perseveres with support | Off task sometimes; effort varies; gives up when challenged | Frequently off task; minimal effort; gives up easily |

### Weekly Participation Score
- 16 points possible per week
- Convert to percentage for gradebook

### Self-Assessment Component
Students complete weekly self-assessment using same rubric. Teacher score and student score are averaged.

## 8. HOW TO USE THESE RUBRICS

### Before the Assignment
1. **Share the rubric** with students before they begin work
2. **Discuss each criterion** and what each level looks like
3. **Show examples** (exemplars) of work at different levels
4. **Answer questions** about expectations

### During the Assignment
1. **Reference the rubric** when students ask questions
2. **Use rubric language** in feedback during drafts/checkpoints
3. **Encourage self-assessment** using the rubric

### Scoring the Assignment
1. **Read/observe** the complete work first before scoring
2. **Score each criterion** independently (don't let one area influence others)
3. **Use the descriptions** - find the level that best matches the work
4. **Be consistent** - calibrate with colleagues when possible

### Providing Feedback
1. **Highlight** 1-2 strengths (specific criteria)
2. **Identify** 1-2 areas for growth (specific criteria)
3. **Provide next steps** - what should the student do differently?
4. **Connect to rubric language** so students understand the assessment

### Modifying Rubrics
These rubrics can be modified for specific assignments:
- **Reduce criteria** for shorter assignments
- **Adjust descriptors** for specific content
- **Change point values** based on emphasis
- **Add criteria** for unique assignment requirements

When modifying, always maintain:
- Clear distinction between levels
- Observable/measurable criteria
- Alignment to standards
- Student-friendly language

### Rubric Quick Reference
| Type | Best For | Key Focus |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Writing (General) | Any writing assignment | Focus, development, voice, conventions |
| Argumentative | Persuasive essays, debates | Claim, evidence, reasoning |
| Narrative | Stories, personal narratives | Structure, character, description |
| Research | Reports, projects | Sources, integration, citations |
| Presentation | Speeches, oral reports | Delivery, visuals, engagement |
| Collaboration | Group projects | Contribution, teamwork, communication |
| Participation | Daily engagement | Engagement, preparation, respect, effort |

## APPENDIX: GRADE-LEVEL EXPECTATIONS

### 6th Grade Focus
- Building foundational skills
- Learning to use rubrics for self-assessment
- Focus on organization and basic conventions
- Developing academic vocabulary

### 7th Grade Focus
- Increasing complexity of evidence and reasoning
- Multiple sources and synthesis
- More sophisticated word choice
- Peer feedback using rubrics

### 8th Grade Focus
- Advanced analysis and evaluation
- Independent application of rubric criteria
- Preparation for high school expectations
- Complex, multi-faceted assessments
