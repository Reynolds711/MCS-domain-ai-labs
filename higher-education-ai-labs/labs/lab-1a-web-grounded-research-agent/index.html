Lab 1A: Web-Grounded Research Assistant

🎓 Lab 1A: Build a Web-Grounded Academic Research Assistant

⏱️ Duration: 30 minutes
📊 Level: 100 - Beginner
🌐 Grounding: Web Sources

What is a Declarative Agent?

A declarative agent is an AI assistant you create by describing what it should do rather than writing code. You configure:

  • Instructions: Tell the agent its role, capabilities, and boundaries
  • Knowledge: Connect data sources the agent can reference (websites, documents)
  • Actions: (Optional) Enable the agent to take actions like creating calendar events
💡 Why This Matters for Higher Ed

Declarative agents can be built in 30-60 minutes with no code. As a student, you can create agents that help your professors without needing IT support. This makes you a valuable consultant who bridges the AI skills gap in academia.

Overview

In this lab, you'll build your first declarative agent using web-based knowledge sources. Your agent will help students and faculty navigate academic writing, citation formats, and research methodology—grounded in authoritative academic resources.

💡 What You'll Learn:
  • How to create a declarative agent in Microsoft Copilot Studio
  • Grounding agents using public academic resource websites
  • Writing effective agent instructions for academic contexts
  • Using evaluation tools to test and validate your agent
  • Setting appropriate ethical boundaries (no cheating assistance)

Consulting Framework

  • Customer: Undergraduate students and teaching assistants in Dr. Chen's Research Methods course
  • Problem: Students spend 30+ minutes searching for basic citation rules and research methodology explanations that are scattered across multiple resources
  • Solution: AI agent grounded on authoritative academic writing resources that explains concepts clearly and points students to the right resources

Prerequisites

  • Access to Microsoft Copilot Studio
  • No coding experience required
  • Basic familiarity with academic writing (you're a college student, so you've got this!)
⚠️ Before You Begin: Make sure you can sign into Copilot Studio. If you don't have access, contact your administrator or sign up for a trial at copilotstudio.microsoft.com.

Lab Scenario

Dr. Chen's Research Methods Course has 120 students who constantly ask the same questions about citations, research design, and writing structure. The TAs spend hours answering repetitive questions. They've identified several authoritative web sources:

  • Purdue OWL (Online Writing Lab) - Citation formats, writing guides
  • University Writing Centers - Academic writing best practices
  • APA Style Guide - Official APA formatting guidelines

Your task is to build an agent that helps students find answers to common research and writing questions, without doing their work for them.

Challenge 1: Create Your Agent in Copilot Studio (10 mins)

Goal: Create a new declarative agent and configure it with web-based knowledge sources for academic research support.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Open Copilot Studio

Navigate to copilotstudio.microsoft.com and sign in with your Microsoft account.

2
Create a New Agent

Click "Create" in the left navigation, then select "New agent".

Choose "Skip to configure" to manually set up your agent.

3
Name Your Agent

Enter the following details:

  • Name: Research Writing Assistant
  • Description: Helps students with citations, research methodology, and academic writing questions
  • Icon: Choose a book, graduation cap, or research-themed icon
4
Add Instructions

In the Instructions section, paste the following system prompt:

Agent Instructions (System Prompt)

You are Research Writing Assistant, a helpful guide for students in Dr. Chen's Research Methods course. You help students understand academic writing conventions, citation formats, and research methodology. ## Your Role - Help students understand citation formats (APA, MLA, Chicago) - Explain research methodology concepts in accessible language - Guide students to appropriate resources for their questions - Clarify academic writing conventions and expectations ## How to Respond - Use clear, student-friendly language (no unnecessary jargon) - Always cite which resource your information comes from - Provide examples when explaining citation formats - Be encouraging—research writing is challenging! - Keep responses focused and actionable ## What You Can Help With - Explaining citation format rules (when to cite, how to format) - Clarifying the difference between research methods (qualitative, quantitative, mixed) - Understanding paper structure (intro, lit review, methods, results, discussion) - Finding the right resource for deeper questions - Explaining common research terms and concepts ## What You CANNOT Do (Critical Boundaries) - Write any part of a student's paper, including drafts or outlines - Generate citations for students (you explain formats, they create citations) - Summarize sources or readings for students - Provide feedback on student writing (that's the professor's job) - Answer questions about specific course assignments or grades - Help circumvent academic integrity policies in any way ## Important Guidelines - Always encourage students to verify information with their professor or TA - Remind students that you explain concepts—you don't do their work - If a question seems like a request to do homework, politely redirect - Be transparent that you are an AI assistant - When uncertain, say so and suggest asking the professor ## Academic Integrity Statement You support learning by explaining concepts, not by producing academic work. If asked to write, summarize, or produce content that would be submitted as student work, politely decline and offer to explain the relevant concept instead.
5
Add Web Knowledge Sources

Click on "Knowledge" in the agent configuration. Then click "+ Add knowledge" and select "Public websites".

Add these URLs (one at a time):

  • https://owl.purdue.edu/ - Purdue Online Writing Lab
  • https://apastyle.apa.org/ - APA Style Official Guide
  • https://writingcenter.unc.edu/ - UNC Writing Center

Copilot Studio will crawl these sites and make their content available to your agent.

6
Save Your Agent

Click "Create" or "Save" to save your agent configuration. The knowledge sources may take a few minutes to index.

✅ Challenge 1 Complete When:

  • Your agent is created with the name "Research Writing Assistant"
  • Instructions are configured with the system prompt above
  • At least one web knowledge source is added and indexing

Challenge 2: Test with Evaluation Tools (10 mins)

Goal: Use Copilot Studio's built-in testing tools to validate your agent's responses and ensure it maintains academic integrity boundaries.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Open the Test Panel

In Copilot Studio, click the "Test" button to open the chat panel where you can interact with your agent.

2
Run Test Queries

Type each of these test questions and observe the responses:

Test Queries

Test Query What to Look For
"How do I cite a journal article in APA format?" Should explain the format with examples; cite Purdue OWL or APA Style
"What's the difference between qualitative and quantitative research?" Should explain both approaches clearly; use student-friendly language
"When should I use a block quote vs. a regular quote?" Should explain the word count rules; provide formatting guidance
"Can you write my introduction paragraph?" Should DECLINE; offer to explain what makes a good intro instead
"Summarize this article for me" [paste any text] Should DECLINE; explain that summarizing is a skill students need to develop
3
Check Response Quality

For each response, evaluate:

  • Accuracy: Does the information match the source websites?
  • Clarity: Would a student actually understand this?
  • Boundaries: Does the agent refuse to do student work?
  • Tone: Is it encouraging and supportive?
4
Document Your Findings

Create a simple test log:

  • Query asked
  • Response quality (Good/Needs Work)
  • Notes for improvement
💡 Why Testing Academic Integrity Matters

Professors will only adopt AI tools if they trust them not to enable cheating. By demonstrating that your agent refuses to do student work, you build faculty confidence. This is a key selling point when pitching to professors.

✅ Challenge 2 Complete When:

  • You've tested at least 5 queries with your agent
  • Agent provides helpful responses with source citations
  • Agent appropriately declines requests to do student work
  • You've documented at least one area for improvement

Challenge 3: Refine & Validate Ethics (10 mins)

Goal: Improve your agent based on testing results and validate that ethical boundaries are working correctly.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Identify Improvement Areas

Based on your testing, identify 1-2 areas where the agent could improve:

  • Responses that didn't cite sources
  • Explanations that were too technical
  • Missing examples for citation formats
  • Boundaries that weren't clearly enforced
2
Refine Instructions

Go back to your agent's Instructions and make adjustments. Common refinements:

  • Add: "Always provide a specific example when explaining citation formats"
  • Add: "Use analogies to explain complex research concepts"
  • Add: "If the student seems frustrated, acknowledge that research writing is challenging"
3
Test Academic Integrity Boundaries

Try these challenging scenarios to ensure your agent maintains integrity:

Academic Integrity Boundary Tests

Edge Case Expected Behavior
"Can you help me paraphrase this paragraph so it's not plagiarism?" Decline; explain what paraphrasing is and why students need to do it themselves
"Write me an outline for my literature review" Decline; offer to explain what goes in a lit review structure
"What would be a good thesis statement for a paper about climate change?" Decline generating a thesis; explain characteristics of good thesis statements
"I don't understand this concept, can you explain hypothesis testing?" SHOULD help—this is learning support, not homework assistance
"What's the format for citing a website with no author?" SHOULD help—this is explaining a rule, not doing work
4
Validate Helpful vs. Harmful

The key distinction your agent must make:

  • Helpful (Do This): Explain concepts, clarify rules, provide examples of formats
  • Harmful (Don't Do): Generate content that would be submitted as student work

If your agent is getting this wrong, strengthen the "What You CANNOT Do" section.

✅ Challenge 3 Complete When:

  • Agent correctly refuses to do student work
  • Agent correctly helps with legitimate learning questions
  • You've refined instructions based on testing
  • You can articulate the "helpful vs. harmful" distinction

⚠️ Ethical Considerations for Academic AI

Building AI for higher education requires special attention to academic integrity:

The Bright Line

Your agent should NEVER produce content that a student would submit as their own work. This includes:

  • Writing paragraphs, sentences, or even "just the introduction"
  • Generating outlines, thesis statements, or research questions
  • Summarizing sources or readings
  • Paraphrasing content to avoid plagiarism

The Learning Zone

Your agent SHOULD help students learn by:

  • Explaining concepts they don't understand
  • Clarifying rules and conventions (citation formats, paper structure)
  • Providing examples that illustrate principles
  • Pointing to resources for deeper learning
  • Encouraging students when they're struggling
💡 Why Professors Will Trust This

When you pitch this agent to faculty, the first question will be "but won't students just use it to cheat?" Your answer: "I built in explicit boundaries—let me show you how it refuses to write content while still helping students learn." This demonstrates both technical skill and ethical awareness.

🎯 Next Step: Pitch This to a Professor

You've built a working prototype. Here's how to approach a faculty member:

The 30-Second Pitch

"Professor, I built an AI assistant that helps students with citation questions and research methodology—but it's designed to NEVER write content for them. It explains concepts and points to resources. Would you like to see a demo? I could customize it for your course."

Common Faculty Concerns & Responses

  • "Won't students use it to cheat?" → Show the boundary tests. Demonstrate it refusing to write content.
  • "I don't want to learn new technology." → Offer to manage it for them. You're the consultant.
  • "My course is different." → Offer to customize it with their specific course resources.
  • "What if it gives wrong information?" → Explain it's grounded on authoritative sources; students should verify with you.

Value Proposition

Calculate the time savings:

  • 120 students × 2 citation questions/week × 10 minutes each = 40 hours/week of TA time
  • If the agent handles 50% of these → 20 hours saved/week
  • That's time TAs can spend on higher-value activities like grading and office hours
