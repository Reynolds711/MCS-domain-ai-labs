Problem: Creating study materials, practice questions, and review guides is time-intensive, especially for large enrollment courses.

Why it matters: Faculty draft study materials faster. Ensures alignment with course learning objectives. Maintains consistent quality across sections.

ROI: 25% increase in writing center throughput

Getting Started

Prerequisites

  • Access to Microsoft Copilot Studio (or compatible AI platform)
  • Basic understanding of declarative agents and AI prompting
  • No coding experience required

Recommended Learning Path

  1. Start with Lab 1A: Web-Grounded Academic Research Assistant (30 mins)
  2. Then Lab 1B: File-Grounded Course Assistant (30 mins)
  3. Propose to a professor: Use your skills to help a faculty member
  4. Advanced labs: Office Hours Triage, Academic Integrity Guide

🎯 How to Pitch AI Agents to Faculty

After completing these labs, here's how to approach professors:

  1. Identify the pain point: "I noticed you get many questions about syllabus policies..."
  2. Show, don't tell: Build a quick prototype using their actual syllabus
  3. Quantify value: "This could save 5+ hours per week of repetitive Q&A"
  4. Address concerns: "The agent only answers from your documents, nothing hallucinated"
  5. Offer to iterate: "I can refine based on feedback from a pilot with one section"

⚠️ Ethical Guidelines for Higher Ed AI Agents

All labs emphasize responsible AI practices:

  • Augment, don't replace: Agents assist learning, never do the work for students
  • Academic integrity: Agents must not help students cheat or plagiarize
  • Transparency: Always disclose when AI is being used
  • Human oversight: Faculty review all AI-assisted processes
  • Equity focus: Ensure agents serve all students, including those with different needs
  • Privacy: Never include actual student data in agent training
